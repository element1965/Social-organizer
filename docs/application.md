# Application and Need protocol

The [Need protocol](narrative/topics/mechanics/need-protocol.md) describes confirmed handshakes, who can report a Need, notification waves, Participation, and regular cycles. Social Organizer records Needs and voluntary Participation and sends notifications. People decide what to do and agree directly in their own chat. The app handles no money and guarantees no outcome. A local or self-hosted instance has its own separate network.

Wording rules for the interface, the landing and the assistant, the open questions for the author and the release checklist are in [docs/release/launch-readiness.md](release/launch-readiness.md); it also holds the scale steps (to 10 thousand, 100 thousand and a million people, each with the sign that starts it and the measurements behind it). In the code and the database a Need is still the `Collection` record and a Participation is the `Obligation` record; those names never reach the interface.

## Web Frontend (apps/web)

React 19 SPA with tRPC client.

### Pages

Route pages other than the guest landing are `React.lazy`. The Circle chunk is prefetched after sign-in. A failed lazy chunk retries the import once; if the preload still fails, the page reloads once (a sessionStorage timestamp stops a loop) and then shows a reload screen. `telegram-web-app.js` is injected during HTML parse, before the app entry, only inside Telegram: `tgWebAppData` or `tgWebApp` in the URL or hash, a Telegram user agent, `window.TelegramWebviewProxy`, sessionStorage `__telegram__initParams`, or an iframe that has those stored params or a `tgWebApp` hash. That still matches after a full-page reload inside Telegram Desktop, iOS, Android, and Web A/K. A normal browser does not request the script.

### 3D Visualization (graph-3d)

`NetworkGraph` (react-force-graph-3d) draws the signed-in Circle. The planet, globe, cloud, and handshake-path scenes are gone. The graph chunk (the force-graph libraries and the Three.js addons they use, about 254 KB / 74 KB gzip) loads when the Circle opens, together with the Three.js core chunk (about 1069 KB / 285 KB gzip) that the guest landing's flock scene also uses.

- *Stable data* — node objects are kept between renders and only a structural change (who is in the graph, names, photos) updates the graph; `connectionCount` / `lastSeen` are applied in place. A parent re-render no longer rebuilds all nodes and restarts the simulation (it was ~8 FPS on 500 nodes).
- *Cheap nodes* — shared textures/materials, sprites instead of spheres, one frame loop for LOD (avatar + label → dot → star-like dot with a minimum on-screen size; near dots are capped at 11 px and photos at 34 px on screen, so the camera passing close never fills the view with one node), labels and photos created lazily within a per-frame budget (500 nodes mount in ~0.4 s instead of ~4.5 s); decorative sprites are excluded from hover raycasting; the pulse ring exists only on nodes that show it.
- *Batched drawing* — all links are one `LineSegments` (per-vertex colour and alpha; the highlighted path is a second small one, curved) and all node dots are one `Points` with a small shader, sorted far to near each frame; per-node sprites stay only as invisible tap/hover targets. A 500-person graph went from ~1100 draw calls per frame to ~22 (CPU per frame 6.4 → 3.3 ms on a desktop). The pixel ratio is capped at 2 and lowered step by step while frames come late (one step only on 30 Hz screens), then raised back. Performance demo: `pnpm --filter @so/graph-3d demo`, open `http://localhost:5199/?n=500` (synthetic network; `window.__SO_GRAPH_DEBUG__` exposes the force graph and our frame time).
- *Shape* — a gentle radial force keeps each handshake depth on its own spherical shell (you in the middle, then 1°, 2°, 3°), so the network forms a round cloud instead of sprawling like a tree (`BALL_STRENGTH`, `BALL_SHELLS`; set the strength to 0 for a free-form layout). The default centering force is removed (it shifted the cloud every tick against the pinned center) and a speed limit (`MAX_NODE_SPEED`) keeps the first ticks from exploding, so nodes settle without jerks.
- *Camera and LOD* — LOD switches with hysteresis (no avatar/dot flicker on a border); nodes right in front of the lens fade out, so the camera passes through the 1° shell instead of getting stuck behind a huge avatar.
- *Memory between visits* — avatar textures live in a shared LRU (`avatarCache.ts`) and the settled layout in `layoutCache.ts`; screens hand the saved camera back as `initialView`, which skips the intro flight. Nodes already shown once are remembered per owner, so coming back to the Circle does not replay the pop-in. Leaving while the layout still moves saves it too, marked unsettled: the next visit starts from there and lets it finish settling instead of freezing it half-way.
- *Photos* — a photo replaces the dot when it would be at least 18 px on screen and goes back below 15 px (a margin, so the orbiting camera does not flip it), stays at least 1.5 s once shown, and at most 40 show at once, the biggest first. The dot grows into the photo in 180 ms and shrinks back before the swap. Photos are fetched ahead from 9 px (60 at most), and at most 4 freshly loaded ones are applied per frame.
- *Theme* — the Circle scene follows the app theme: in the light theme the graph uses `GRAPH_COLORS_LIGHT` (the pale scene, solid dots that step back by colour, not by opacity: ink for the 1st handshake, the `--node-2` grey for the 2nd, a grey derived from `--ink-muted` and `--node-3` for the 3rd, so every dot keeps at least 3:1 on the ground; links me ↔ 1st handshake and path links in the care teal at 4:1; ink names a step bolder with a wider halo than in the dark scene; path names in `--care-hover`, 6:1; a white centre glow; NetworkGraph `sceneTheme`, `depthAlpha` in `palette.ts`; the measured ratios are in its comments). A theme switch repaints everything in place, the far-ring dust included, and the scene tokens (`--so-scene*`, `--so-node-*`, `--so-care-scene`) turn light inside `.scene-follows-theme` on the Circle screen only, so the header, ring chips and status bar text stay readable. The landing hero, figures and the QR camera keep the dark scene.
- *Layers and names* ? photos and the "me" tile write depth only inside the disc / tile (`alphaTest`), and the dot layer draws after the photo sprites. The graph places names every sixth frame in priority order (me, the focused person, the rest of the highlighted path, 1st handshake, 2nd handshake), nearest first within each group. Other names need 3 px of clearance from the centre tile and make room for earlier names and, for the 1st and 2nd handshakes, nearer photos. A hidden name needs extra clearance to enter, while a placed name tolerates minor overlap with names and photos. When their nodes can be projected, the "me" and focused names try four sides within the usable canvas. If all anchors collide, they use a viewport-clamped fallback that may overlap. The phone reserves space for the measured header (`insetTop`) and ring chips (`labelInsetBottom`) and fades the scene behind both. Names fade in and out (180 ms), and a name that just left the nearest few remains eligible for up to 1.5 s. The hover tooltip is off on touch screens (`hover: none`).
- *1st handshake* — the nearest circle reads first: its dots are the largest and carry an accent rim (DotLayer `rim` attribute), its photos an accent ring (`rimMaterial`), the links between me and it are drawn in the accent colour (LinkLayer spokes; they step back while a path is highlighted), and the 2nd and 3rd handshakes are drawn quieter. Names and photos favour it: a 2nd-handshake name competes as if it were 1.5× farther (`RING2_LABEL_PENALTY`), 1st-handshake photos rank as if 35% bigger (`RING1_PHOTO_BONUS`), and in the overlap pass its names keep their place before the 2nd handshake's. Up close more names are allowed (8 in a wide view, up to 16), and a new name shows only after it passed the overlap check, so newly created names wait for placement.
- *Navigation* — two taps on empty space zoom in (`DOUBLE_TAP_ZOOM`); pinch, the wheel and the zoom buttons work as before. The person card steps through the people of the same handshake in name order (arrows with "3 of 9", or a sideways swipe on the card, mirrored right-to-left; it wraps around), and the camera flies to each one. A selected person is framed together with the whole chain from "me" to them: the camera looks at the chain side-on, picking among 160 directions the one where no two people in the chain cover each other, near the current view; the distance fits the chain into the part of the screen above the card (72–240 scene units, so neither a single tile nor the whole cloud fills the view); auto-rotation stays off while a person is selected and eases back in after the card is closed.
- *Motion* — "bloom": nodes start next to their parent and unfold with low friction (glide/overshoot) while popping in (staggered by depth); gentle floating and a synchronized online pulse; hover grows a node; orbit controls with damping (inertia after a fling), auto-rotate that eases back in after 2.5 s of inactivity; the camera frames the whole graph and flies along an arc (own tween, cancelled as soon as the user touches the view); buttons "Focus on me" and "Show whole network".
- *Safety* — names in the hover tooltip are HTML-escaped; WebGL teardown is deferred so a StrictMode double effect cannot kill the renderer; the simulation is never reheated before the library has digested the data (that crashed the render loop on remount — black screen on Android). A failed scene (no WebGL context, a lost GPU, a chunk that did not load) is caught by `GraphErrorBoundary` in `CircleGraph.tsx`: only the scene shows "The graph did not open on this device" with Retry, the feed, lists and navigation keep working.

### Frontend Technologies

- **State:** Zustand (auth, theme) + tRPC React Query (server data). Polling: one light "what changed" request, `notification.pulse`, every 20 s from the shell (`hooks/usePulse.ts`), instead of a timer per list. It returns counters and times only, in groups (notices, incoming and own requests, confirmations, own Needs, Needs the person takes part in, milestones, open circle questions, own connection count); when a group differs from the previous answer, exactly the lists behind it are invalidated (`notification.list` / `unreadCount` / `attention`, `pending.incoming` / `myPending` / `acceptedRecent`, `collection.myActive` / `myParticipating`, `milestone.list`, `moderation.myOpenReviews`, `connection.getCount`). A group the server could not read this time comes back empty (null) and keeps its previous value, so one failing query does not stop the other lists. Every 5 minutes all of these lists are re-read anyway, as a safety net for what no stamp stands for (a milestone reached through other people's connections, a changed name or photo). The author opening their own Need records it with raw SQL, so `collections.updatedAt`, which the pulse watches, does not move. The lists still load on mount and after mutations; nothing polls while signed out. The Circle's lists (`notification.list` / `unreadCount` / `attention`, `pending.incoming` / `myPending`, `collection.myActive` / `myParticipating`, `milestone.list`) keep a 30 s `staleTime` (`lib/circleLists.ts`), so coming back to the Circle within 30 s shows them from memory; every settled mutation marks them stale without refetching, so a list is read again on its next mount after any action of the person, as before. One open Circle screen went from about 16–18 requests a minute to 3 (plus the 5-minute network refresh). The heavy network queries (`graphSlice`, `connection.list`, `getNetworkStats`, `networkCapabilities`) are re-read every 5 minutes with a 60 s `staleTime`; a change in `connection.getCount` (re-read when the pulse sees the count move) refreshes them at once (`components/circle/network.ts`). Short polls that live only while one screen is open (QR, invite intro status, a Need page, support chat) keep their own timers. The single retry waits 1–3 s at random, and the refresh after an outage is spread over 4 s
- **Routing:** React Router v7. Guest `/` stays eager; other pages are lazy behind a small spinner
- **UI:** ui2 components + semantic tokens in `styles/tokens.css`. A few older screens still use the legacy Tailwind ramps
- **i18n:** i18next v25 + react-i18next (28 languages), auto-detect via navigator.language
- **Icons:** local SVG icons in `ui2` and feature components, plus social network marks
- **QR:** qrcode.react (the scanner chunk loads jsQR on `/add/scan`). Every code carries the brand tile in the centre (`lib/qrLogo.ts`, error correction level H)
- **Backup:** @so/gun-backup (Gun.js + IndexedDB), started from the signed-in shell

## Key Features

- **Sending a link in one tap** — next to the system Share button the Add card and the Need page have "WhatsApp" and "Telegram" (`components/share/MessengerButtons.tsx`, `lib/messengerShare.ts`): the messenger opens with the text and the link filled in (`wa.me/?text=…`, `t.me/share/url`), the person picks the chat. Invite links sent this way carry `?src=wa` / `?src=tg`, so the admin funnel shows which messenger brings people. Inside the Telegram Mini App the Telegram link stays in Telegram (`openTelegramLink`).
- **Personal invitations in a batch** — Add → "Personal invitations" (`components/add/NamedInvites.tsx`): the person types names of people they know, one per line, and each gets its own link with `?to=Name` and the message "Name, Anna is inviting you…", sent by hand through WhatsApp, Telegram or the system sheet. Up to 30 names, duplicates and anything that is not a name are dropped (`safeAddressee` in `@so/shared`, the same rule the invite page and its preview use). The names never leave the screen: nothing is sent to the server and no address book is read.
- **Need page for people outside the network** — `GET /sos/:id` is answered by the API with `index.html` carrying its own link card (`findSosPreview` / `renderSosPreview` in `invite-preview.service.ts`): the same headline as the page ("Anna needs assistance"), the author's photo, and a line by state (open: joining confirms you know the person; amount reported; closed; paused). No amount, description or participants in the card: it stays in chats long after the Need is over. A guest opening a real Need page is counted once per browser session under the source `need` (`invite.opened` with `kind: 'need'`). The page links to "How Social Organizer works" in every state, so a closed Need is not a dead end. An open Need says what taking part means before anyone signs in ("If you take part": you name an amount, from 1 $; the author can provide a chat link; money, if any, goes directly to the author and nothing is paid here). "Take part" signs a guest in on the same page (`?join=1`, with the author's photo and name), then opens the Need in the app without a page reload. Opening it confirms that you know the author personally and adds you to the author’s circle; Google and Apple return through their callback pages, and the installed app restores the Need after its deep-link sign-in too. A newcomer goes straight to the Need (through the name step only after an e-mail sign-up); after the first Participation the Need shows "What happens now": the author sees it, a chat link if one is available, a handshake if opening the Need earned the first connection milestone, an offer to keep the amount as the intention ("Not now" keeps the current one; the intention is never changed on the side) and "See how the network works", which opens the intro. Minimums: Participation at least 1 $, intention 0 ("I can't take part right now") or at least 1 $ (`MIN_OBLIGATION_AMOUNT`, `MIN_INTENTION_AMOUNT` in `@so/shared`). A participant of an open Need gets a card "Pass this Need on to others who know its author" with the same share buttons the author has; the author's Share now uses the native sheet in the installed app.
- **How the people who came through me are doing** — above the list on the Add screen: how many of the person's direct connections already have `ASK_MIN_DIRECT` connections of their own. Shown to the owner of the circle only; no order and no comparison between people.
- **You may know each other** — the Add screen offers people at the 2nd handshake who share two or more acquaintances with the person (`suggestion.service.ts`, `connection.suggestions`, `components/add/SuggestList.tsx`), most shared first, at most 8, with the count and through whom. The block starts folded to the first three, with an "N more" button for the rest, so a long list does not stretch the screen. They are already visible by name (rings 1–3), so nothing new is shown. "We know each other" sends the usual request, which the other person confirms; the cross hides a person on this device. Never offered: someone the two already have a request with in any state (waiting, declined, withdrawn), an ignore entry on either side, a deleted account, anyone at the 150 limit. The block is absent when there is nobody to offer.
- **Where a connection came from, and the admin funnel** — every request and connection keeps its source: `link` (personal link; `link_tg` / `link_wa` when the shared address carried `?src=tg` / `?src=wa` or the invite opened in the Telegram Mini App), `qr` (live QR), `shake`, `need` (a public Need page), `direct` (a person page), `suggest`, `bot` (the bot kept a lost invite). `?src=` is remembered on the device until the request is sent, because sign-in drops the query string (`lib/inviteSrc.ts`). A guest opening an invite page is counted once per browser session and link (`invite.opened` → `invite_open_days`); messenger previews do not run the page and are not counted. Admin statistics (`/me/stats`) has a Funnel tab (`funnel.service.ts`, `stats.adminFunnel`): people by the week they signed up over the last 8 weeks (opened an invite, signed up, sent a request, first connection, 5 connections, Need open), requests and connections by source, and newcomers with a connection per active person for 7 and 28 days. Counts only, no names. Nothing of this is shown to other people. Local checks: `apps/api/scripts/check-growth.ts` and `apps/api/scripts/check-share-preview.ts` (the Need link card).
- **Shake to connect** — two people shake their phones at the same moment on any screen of the app. Each phone calls `shake.register`; the server (`services/shake.service.ts`, in memory, single API instance) pairs shakes that arrived within 3 s of each other (a shake keeps searching for 6 s) from the same public IP or close geolocation (sent only if the person already allowed it, never prompted), waiting 0.5 s so a third shake nearby turns the result into "crowded" (use the QR code) instead of a wrong match. Both see the other person and confirm; then they are connected in person (`connectInPersonTx`, the same path as the live QR, within the 150 limit). Closing the sheet declines. After any new connection (shake, QR scan, confirmed request) `apps/web/src/lib/refreshNetwork.ts` refetches the circle, graph, network figures and milestones at once, on both phones. The new person is drawn on the first ring right away and a "joining" card at the top of the Circle feed (pulsing avatar, progress line, the line to them lit on the graph) stays until the graph slice with their network is back, then says how much the network grew (`components/circle/joining.ts`, `JoinCard.tsx`); the slice starts loading at the moment of the join, before the Circle opens. The other phone learns of the join from the cheap connection count: every 4 s while the live QR is shown (with a banner), every 20 s on the Circle. Detector: `apps/web/src/hooks/useShakeDetector.ts`; overlay: `components/shake/ShakeProvider.tsx` (ignored while typing, while the QR camera is on or while a sheet is open). iOS: in the native app the first tap anywhere silently asks for motion access (Capacitor grants it without a dialog); in a plain mobile browser the Add and QR screens show an "Enable shake" button. Two shakes with no shared network (same IPv4, or the same IPv6 /64 for phones on one Wi-Fi) and no close geo are paired only when they are the only two shakes in the window, and such a pair is blind: both phones show the same three-symbol code and neither person is shown — no name, photo or "already connected" — until both confirm that the code matches on each other's screen; a different code declines and who it was stays unknown. `status` and `respond` are rate-limited per person. Geolocation counts only with an accuracy of 200 m or better, and two fixes match within 150–500 m, so a claimed coarse position cannot pair strangers across a city. "Already connected" is read once, before anyone answers, so a pair this shake just connected is not reported as already connected; a 150-limit error is worded for each of the two. Local checks: `apps/api/scripts/check-shake-matcher.ts` (matcher) and `apps/api/scripts/check-connect-flows.ts` (personal link, live QR and shake end to end against a local database)
- **Start-up loader** — `index.html` holds an inline CSS loader inside `#root` (brand mark with two rings of orbiting dots, light/dark from the early theme class, static with reduced motion) that shows before the bundle loads; `components/ui2/AppLoader.tsx` renders the same picture as the start-up Suspense and sign-in fallback, continues its animation and fades out over the first screen; after 8 s it adds "Still loading…"
- **One unit — $** — all amounts are entered, stored and displayed in $ (conditional dollars, 1 $ = 1 unit of participation); no currency choice and no conversion
- **Connection count** — displayed next to every user throughout the app
- **Handshake path** — shown when viewing profiles and collections of non-direct connections
- **Network reach** — real-time calculation of how many people will receive notifications (min of amount or reachable users)
- **1:1 notification ratio** — amount entered = number of people notified
- **Localized statuses** — ACTIVE/BLOCKED/CLOSED/CANCELLED translated in all 28 languages
- **Dark/Light theme** — system preference detection + manual toggle (auto-synced with Telegram theme in Mini App mode)
- **Contact links** — all social network contact types (Telegram, Instagram, Twitter, LinkedIn, VK, Facebook, WhatsApp, Email, Website) open correct URLs via `buildContactUrl`
- **TG username auto-save** — Telegram username automatically saved/updated as contact on each login
- **Online status indicator** — green dot + "Online" when last seen within 5 minutes, gray dot + relative time otherwise; auto-updated on every authenticated API call
- **Hide contacts** — off by default; users can hide their contacts from non-connected users; direct connections and notified users still see them
- **Connection nicknames** — custom names for connections (like a phone book), editable from the person screen; the nickname replaces the real name across the circle, people, and collections
- **Telegram Mini App** — calls `ready()` and `expand()` once, auto-signs in via `initData`, and supports BackButton navigation and tab haptics. It follows Telegram's light or dark setting, hides the app theme control, and applies the current canvas token to the host background and header on supported clients.
- **Onboarding** — auto-shown for new users, completable flag in database
- **My intention** — people can set the amount they are usually ready to name for one Need from `/me/stake`
- **AI Chat Assistant** — a screen at `/me/help/assistant` (there is no floating button). It supports text and voice input. A failed send keeps the text with a Retry
- **FAQ** — admin-managed FAQ at `/me/help/faq`; admins can create, edit, and delete questions, with LLM auto-translation

## Terminology (Glossary)

| Term | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Handshake** | Mutual confirmation of an existing meaningful connection between two people |
| **Need** | One of the two signals: a participant's message about what they need (amount, Emergency or Regular — every 28 days, a chat link). The `Collection` record |
| **Participation** | The other signal: a participant's message about their own voluntary participation, with an amount they name. A voluntary intention, not a payment. The `Obligation` record |
| **Handshake chain** | Path of connections between two users through mutual acquaintances |
| **Connection count** | Number of first-level connections (handshakes) a user has |
| **Declared participation of the network** | Sum of the intentions of the people **up to the 6th handshake**: the farthest a Need can reach. The headline "N people in your network" counts the same people: rings 1–3 exactly plus one rounded count of the 4th to 6th (the far chip). The Circle, Profile, My intention and the Need screen (amount hint, "N people can see it") show the same two numbers. The name is never shortened: in the Circle header it has a line of its own under the two numbers, in the Profile row it wraps to two lines, and both open the same explanation sheet (`circle/NetFigureSheet.tsx`). On a person's page the two numbers under each face in the handshake chain are named by a legend (people icon: in their circle; coin: intention) and read out in words to screen readers |
| **My intention** | Amount a user intends to name in one Need of someone they know (whole $, stored in `monthlyBudget`). Participation starts at $1, and the form shows the saved intention even when it is $0. Called "Minimum participation" until 12 Nov 2026; since the launch audit it has one name everywhere: the Profile row (with the line "The amount you are ready to name when someone you know reports a Need"), its screen and field, and the hint on a Need ("My intention: 5 $. You can name more."). The Profile row "Activity" (formerly "Needs and Participation") opens the history |

## Redesign

The redesign replaced the old dashboard, network page, and planet landing with four tabs: Circle, Ask, Add, and Profile. Guests see the public site at `/` and the explainer at `/how`. See the [redesign archive](archive/README.md) for working documents.

The landing (`pages/LandingPage.tsx`, imperative parts in `components/landing/`) tells one story while the reader scrolls, over a fixed 3D flock of starlings: a starling watches only six or seven neighbours, yet the flock moves as one; people are connected the same way (4.57 handshakes between two random Facebook users, with a link to the study), both in one beat, so the reader stops once for them; then five steps: a network of people you know, a Need (for example, Maria has lost her job and is $300 short of the rent; the Need reaches up to 300 people nearest first), everyone decides for themselves (the chain you → Oleg → Maria), a little from many (an intention from $1, not a payment), people agree directly in the chat (Lena writes that the shop where she works is hiring; the app never touches money), "tomorrow it could be you", and the same people as the app shows them: at the end the flock gathers into the app's graph (rings of dots around you, your acquaintances first, the app's dark scene instead of the sky), and on the way to the last beat the graph turns twice round its axis through you, following the scroll (the labels beside it wait until it stops); the last beat shows a Need travelling to you along the chain. Names and sums are the reader's: each language has its own three names, and the shortfall stays in dollars, as the app counts, but follows the price level of the browser's region (from $100 in India to $600 in Norway or Switzerland, $300 in Russia, $500 in the US; `components/landing/money.ts`); as a Need reaches up to one person per dollar, "up to N people" and the counters follow the same number. After the story: an interactive growth model (each person invites R people on average; below 1 the network dies out, above it grows on its own), what comes next (tradespeople, shared projects; on a phone its network fills what the words leave of one screen, and the app section fits one screen too, so each takes one stop), the experiment ("Write to the team" opens the bot with `?start=team`, share, three ways to help, links to Andrei Lubalin's report and the code), questions about how risk sharing works and the app (three steps, store badges, a QR to `/get` on desktop). The first screen ends with a line of facts, and its "open source" links to the repository. The scene (`components/landing/scene.ts`, Three.js, loaded with a dynamic import) draws 2000 to 6500 instanced birds with fixed neighbours; the scroll position sets the story time, one camera shot per phase of the scene with a damped follow (a beat may tell two phases: the first runs through the starling and the people by its middle), a photographed sky that moves with the camera, counters and name tags; the graph beats lay the birds out on the app's shells by handshake (ring 1 to 3, then a dust) and draw them as dots, links and the care tile. `glide.ts` finishes a wheel or key scroll at the nearest rest point in its direction (the middle of a beat, the top of a section, the last screen of a tall section) and never pulls the reader back; inside a section taller than the screen nothing pulls. On touch screens it scrolls the page itself: a swipe in the story moves one beat, and a fling stops at the next rest point ahead, so a fast swipe cannot skip half the story or a section (taps, horizontal drags, pinch, a zoomed page and form controls stay native). It is off under reduced motion. `grow.ts` and `nextNet.ts` are Canvas 2D. Everything stops and is disposed when the page unmounts. The copy lives under `landing.v2` in every locale (Russian is the original; the other 27 languages were each reviewed by two more models before release), and the counter captions are plural families (`_one`, `_few`, …) so the words agree with the number; the page stays dark in both themes (its colours are the `.lv` tokens in `styles/tokens.css`) and mirrors for right-to-left languages. `/how` keeps its own order (a new person, handshake rings, the path) and ends with the closest real example: China's online mutual aid platforms (Xianghubao and others, closed in 2021), side by side with Social Organizer in a table.

Public links can select any of the 28 supported languages with `?lang=pt` or another supported code. The browser saves that display preference. The built server localizes the metadata for the landing, How, Groups and legal pages, with all language alternatives in the HTML and sitemap.

`GET /api/landing/people` returns the count of non-deleted members with at least one non-deleted connection partner, cached for ten minutes. The landing formats the date in the reader's language and keeps a dated snapshot if the request fails. The opening photo is a responsive WebP poster. The Three.js scene loads as a story beat approaches the viewport. Google sign-in on the web is a full-page redirect to Google's account chooser (no Google script on the page). The web manifest names the Google Play app (`related_applications` with `prefer_related_applications: true`), so Chrome on Android offers the store app instead of installing the site; Chrome on a computer, where there is no store app, can still install the site.

Arabic and Hebrew set `dir="rtl"` on the document. Layout spacing and navigation arrows follow the reading direction. The feed counters, input placeholders and 12 px tab labels share the accessible secondary text token.

Motion: CSS only (no animation library), in `apps/web/src/styles/motion.css` plus Tailwind transitions, one easing (`ease-so`) and three lengths (150 ms presses and toggles, about 240 ms things that appear or leave, 320 ms sheets). Every screen's content rises in on mount (`Screen`); tabs (`Segmented`) slide one highlight to the chosen option; the People list slides in from the side of the handshake it came from and its rows come in one after another (`Group stagger`, also History and the offline list); sheets and dialogs fade their backdrop and slide or shrink out on close (`usePresence` in `components/ui2/presence.ts` keeps them mounted for the exit); banners leave upwards; a swiped feed row eases back or flies off, and dismissed rows and the "Get the app" card fold away (`.so-collapse`); the switch knob stretches while pressed; buttons, chips and tappable rows react to the press; the active tab icon bumps and badges pop in; Meter segments grow; input errors fade in. The language chip on the site, on sign-in, and in Profile opens one `LanguageSheet` (a bottom sheet on phones, a dialog on wide screens) instead of the native select. The Circle feed is split by meaning: Waiting for you (requests, acquaintance requests), Your Needs (own Needs and their status rows, a flag icon), You are in (Needs you take part in, a hand icon), People. On desktop the scene has zoom, Back to me, and an expand button that hides the feed column (Escape brings it back). People rows on the 1st handshake say how many more people are in a person's circle besides you ("36 more in their circle", "Only you in their circle so far"); rings 2-3 show the path through your acquaintances, wrapping to a second line when it is long. The saved intention (`remainingBudget`, what "Declared participation of the network" sums) sits at the row end with a small "Intention" caption. The list sorts by Newest (default, with the "Recent" block on the 1st handshake), Name, Intention or, on the 1st handshake, Circle size; one line under the ring switch says what the ring is (people you know personally; one person between you; two people between you); the choice is kept on the device (`so.people.sort`). A horizontal swipe on the list switches between the 1st, 2nd and 3rd handshake (mirrored in right-to-left languages; touches within 24 px of the screen edge are left to the system back gesture, and a mostly vertical move only scrolls). The name search covers every handshake: matches from the other rings follow under their ring titles, and when the open ring has none the list says so before them. The ring chips (1st / 2nd / 3rd) open the People list of that ring everywhere, on the expanded graph too; showing or hiding a ring on the graph is the switch in that list, and Back returns to the expanded graph. On phones the collapsed Events pill can be pulled up: the feed rises with the finger and opens past a 40 px pull; a tap still opens it.

Empty screens (`ui2/EmptyState.tsx`) say what will appear and offer one next step: Activity Open ("Report a Need" or "See how to unlock", depending on access), Activity Past (the Open tab is above), an empty handshake on People (1st: "Add a person"; 2nd and 3rd: "Remind", the path sheet's message, or "Add a person" while the 1st is empty too), the Need's participants (the author gets "Share"; a participant has the Report Participation button right above), "No Participation together yet" on a person's page, the quiet Circle feed ("Add a person"), Add (the Share button above) and an empty FAQ ("Ask the assistant"). Interface strings never guess a gender: no "(а)", "/ла" or "(e)" forms, no gendered word for the reader or for another person, and `{{name}}` stays in the nominative throughout the interface, including the share text, public Need page, link card and moderation feed.
