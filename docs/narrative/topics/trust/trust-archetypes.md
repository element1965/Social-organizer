# Characters as participant archetypes

> Summary: Case's characters (Copycat, Always Cheat, Detective, Grudger, Copykitten…) mapped onto SO participants, for abuse-case thinking, onboarding and storytelling.

## Context

Use these archetypes when you review a mechanic ("what would Always Cheat do with this?"), when you
design onboarding ("we want participants to be nice but not naive") and when you explain why
SO does not need a judge. They are thinking tools, not labels for real people: SO never shows or
assigns a type to anyone.

## Details

| Character | In SO it looks like | What SO gives the others | What SO must not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Always Cooperate** (naive giver) | Takes part in every Need without looking | The chain and the chat to check first; the reminder that taking part is optional | Ask people to be naive. In Case's world naive givers feed cheaters first. |
| **Always Cheat** (moocher) | Reports Needs, never takes part, or takes and disappears | The chain to ask about them; the participants' chat; circle moderation while the Need is open; small shares, so one person's loss is small. Note: today others see only "Participation reported" and "Marked as received by authors", the block is hidden at zero, and the number of Needs reported is not shown, so a person who only asks looks like a newcomer (see open questions) | Hide history, or let a person with no network reach many people (the access rule exists for this: 5 direct connections and 10 people in the network before the first Need). |
| **Copycat** (reciprocator) | Starts with a small Participation, then answers what others do | History of others; reversible Participation | Punish reciprocity as "unkind". |
| **Copykitten** (forgiving reciprocator) | Forgives one misstep, reacts to a pattern | Facts without verdicts; time to talk in the chat | Add automatic penalties that react to one mistake. |
| **Grudger** (never forgives) | A crowd that writes someone off forever after one story | — | Build global bans, public blacklists or ratings. SO's ignore list is private and reversible. |
| **Detective** (probes, then exploits) | Tests the network with Needs to see whether anyone checks | The step "find out for yourself" in the story; history visible to the people deciding | Make it look like nobody ever checks. |
| **Simpleton / Random** (noise) | Inattentive participants, mistakes | Changeable Participation, one notification, the chat to clarify | Treat noise as fraud. |

### The target participant

**Nice, but not naive.** The participant starts by cooperating with something small, checks the
circumstances through people they know, and reacts to what they see in the history and in the
chat. The video and onboarding should show this person, not a donor who gives to everyone.

### The failed cooperation case (from the 2026-11-17 brainstorm)

A builder takes a €5,000 advance and does not do the work. The person who lost the money can
report a Need to cover the loss. Participants decide for themselves whether to take part, and the
story may become known to people connected with both sides. Two processes run in parallel: the
loss is spread among volunteers, and the social consequences become visible. Nobody rules who is
right. This is the archetypes at work: Always Cheat wins once and becomes visible; others react
like Copycats or Copykittens, each on their own judgement. Wording rule: the possibility to
share a loss is not a guarantee of compensation, and visibility does not exclude repeat fraud.
