# Direct cooperation — the natural continuation (next stage)

> Summary: Andrei Lubalin's reasoning (2026-11-17) on why direct cooperation grows out of mutual risk sharing — finding partners in the graph, the €5,000 failed-deal example, no central arbiter — and the precision limits.

## Context

Not part of the current video or landing. It explains to builders and donors why SO is more than a
tool for emergencies. Source: brainstorm messages of 2026-11-17 ([sources](../meta/sources.md)).

## Details

### 1. Protection comes first

A person takes part in covering other people's risks because they want their own life to be
predictable. A referential graph, activity history and the habit of direct contact form.

### 2. The first circle stops being the limit

You need a builder, a doctor, a project partner or a flat to rent. Before, you asked friends, went
to intermediaries or used commercial platforms. Now you can find candidates in the graph, see the
chain of acquaintances and the history of their activity. Friendly prices, direct agreements and
joint initiatives already exist inside separate communities; here they can spread through one
social fabric.

### 3. The risk of cooperation changes

You agree on a renovation. The builder takes a €5,000 advance and does not do the work. The fraud
happened and the loss is real. Now you can report a Need for compensation of these €5,000 to your
network. Participants decide independently whether to take part in covering the loss. The story
of the failed cooperation may become known to people connected with both sides. Two processes run
in parallel: the material consequences of the loss are spread among many volunteers; the
reputational consequences reach the person whose actions caused the loss. Both happen without a
central arbiter.

### 4. Nobody decides who is right

SO does not judge, establish truth, assign a reliability rating or impose sanctions. One person
reports a Need; others find out the circumstances, talk and decide. The spread of information
creates reputational feedback but does not guarantee that every fraud is discovered or that all
participants judge it the same way. Reputation here is a result of social relationships, not a
mark set by the platform.

### 5. Why cooperation becomes natural

> In an environment of mutual risk sharing, direct cooperation becomes rationally more attractive,
> because its possible material consequences can be spread among many participants and the
> history of interactions becomes socially visible.

A person no longer has to limit cooperation to direct acquaintances or use intermediaries only to
reduce risk. The mechanism reproduces itself: more cooperation → more history → more information
for the next decisions → more direct cooperation (the expected autocatalytic dynamic).

### Precision limits

- The possibility to compensate a loss is not a guarantee of compensation.
- Reputational consequences do not exclude repeat fraud.
- Fully excluding significant losses would require proving that counter-participation is
  sufficient in every case. We do not claim that.
- The goal is not to prevent every dishonest act by administrative control, but to create an
  environment where its material consequences can be spread and its social consequences become visible.

### Product note

Today a loss from a failed deal can already be reported as an ordinary Need (amount + chat). The
app has no special "failed deal" type, no accusation feature and no partner search beyond
profiles and chains (the old skills matching is switched off in the MVP, `apps/api/src/services/mvp-scope.ts`). Building such features is a separate decision.
