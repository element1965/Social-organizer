# Invitational coordination — the theory behind SO (Lubalin, 2026)

> Summary: Andrei Lubalin's core distinction — coercive vs invitational interaction by the consequence of refusal, the two coordination mechanisms (command → control → coercion; idea → consensus → assistance) and the causal sequence from mechanism to social environment — and how it meets Case's "the game defines the players".

## Context

Source: Andrei Lubalin, PhD (Sociology), *From Human Behaviour to Social Coordination. Report on a
Completed Theoretical and Engineering Research Programme and Its End-User Implementation*, July 2026,
Zenodo https://zenodo.org/records/21629935, mirrored on the P2P Foundation wiki
(https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/From_Human_Behaviour_to_Social_Coordination). This is the
theoretical foundation of Social Organizer. Use it with builders, researchers and donors; for a
first contact use [what SO is](../core/what-so-is.md).

## Details

### The change of question

Most explanations of social order start from observed behaviour (competition, obedience, distrust,
cooperation) and call it human nature. Lubalin reverses the direction: behaviour observed in an
environment was already selected by that environment's conditions, so it cannot explain them.
The study starts from **interaction between subjects** and leaves the subject's inner nature
outside its boundary.

### Two types of interaction, by the consequence of refusal

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Coercive** | Refusal entails a consequence imposed (or credibly kept imposable) by the initiating subject. |
| **Invitational** | The addressed subject can refuse without an imposed consequence. |

The test concerns a specific action and relation, not its purpose, morality, legality or benefit:
a beneficial proposal is still coercive if refusal is punished.

### Two mechanisms of coordinating a plurality

| Mechanism | Elements |
|---|---|
| **command → control → coercion** | A command specifies the required action; control checks correspondence; coercion makes refusal consequential. |
| **idea → consensus → assistance** | An idea proposes a common course without punishing refusal; consensus is the match of independent acceptances; assistance is voluntary action that realises it. |

Feedback is needed by both. Digital technology did not invent feedback; it changed its scale, so
global distributed coordination through recorded relations became possible without a centre that
gathers information and allocates action.

### The causal sequence

> mechanism of social coordination → conditions of interaction → behavioural strategies of
> subjects and their plurality → reproducible collective behaviour → observable social environment

- A **coercive environment**: refusal is consequential, action is determined asymmetrically, access
  is controlled; compliance without agreement, concealment and competition for control become
  rational. Hierarchy is a property of the environment, not proof of a human preference for it.
- An **invitational environment**: refusal carries no imposed consequence, coordination runs
  through consensus, initiative is distributed, assistance comes from accepted common action.
  Participation is rational where people see an individual benefit; no moral transformation,
  universal altruism or central enforcement is required.

### Where it meets *The Evolution of Trust*

Case ends with "what the game is defines what the players do". Lubalin gives the same direction a
full causal chain and a criterion: SO is a mechanism of the second type, so it must keep every
step **invitational**. Case explains why trust grows under repeat interactions, win-wins and low
noise; Lubalin explains which kind of coordination mechanism produces an environment in which those
strategies become rational. Use them together in [trust conditions in SO](../trust/trust-conditions-in-so.md).

### The invitational test (for any feature or text)

Can a person refuse this — a Need, a Participation, an invitation, a reminder — **without any
consequence imposed by someone else**? If refusing costs them a mark, a rank, a lost access or a
public label, the feature is coercive and does not belong in SO.
