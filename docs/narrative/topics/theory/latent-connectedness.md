# Latent global connectedness and the conditions of its activation

> Summary: The empirical premise (the world's social graph is already connected — 4.74 handshakes between two Facebook users in 2011, 4.57 in 2016, fewer than four people in between), the motivation (a calculable benefit of spreading one person's loss among many), the four conditions of activation, and what Social Organizer does and does not do as a "social utility".

## Context

Second part of Lubalin's report (sections 1, 5 and 7; source in
[invitational coordination](invitational-coordination.md)). It answers the research question:
*what conditions are necessary and sufficient for existing latent global social connectedness to
manifest as a functioning global P2P environment of mutual assistance?*

## Details

### The empirical premise: the world is already connected

- Facebook in 2011, about 721 million users and 69 billion connections: 99.91% of users in one
  connected component, average distance **4.74** links, that is 3.74 people in between (Backstrom
  et al., 2012, "Four degrees of separation").
- Facebook in 2016, 1.59 billion active users: average distance **4.57** links, that is **3.57**
  people in between (Edunov et al., 2016, "Three and a half degrees of separation").

In SO's words a handshake is one link, so: 4.74 handshakes in 2011, 4.57 in 2016, fewer than four
people in between. Note: the report gives "4.74" and "3.57" as the same measure; they are not (3.57
is the number of people in between, checked against the Facebook Research post on 2026-11-18).

Global horizontal connectedness exists and does not need to be built. It is **latent**: the paths
are real, but they do not coordinate mutual assistance beyond each person's immediate relations.
The task is to activate it, not to construct, replace or reorganise it.

### The motivation: a calculable benefit

People take part because they can spread the potential loss of one among many through reciprocal
readiness to assist. Participation is rational when `net(participation) > net(alternative)`, and
the current size of the referential graph is part of that comparison (it sets the potential
coverage a newcomer gets). The benefit is the purpose of participation, not a by-product.

### Self-organization through bounded local fields

Each person formalises only their own existing significant relations, a bounded local set. The sum
of these relations is a referential graph that extends beyond everyone's immediate circle. The
global reach comes from the already global social fabric, not from any new global structure, so
the number of participants is theoretically unlimited.

### The four conditions of activation

1. **Calculable net benefit** relative to an available alternative, at the current scale of the graph.
2. **Formalisation of existing significant relations** through invitation and **independent
   bilateral acceptance**: entry through a relation that already means something to both people,
   never anonymous aggregation.
3. **Referential inheritance**: the path through which each person entered stays visible, so the
   source of a request from beyond your circle can be identified through the chain.
4. **A bounded local field**: no person needs global cognitive or coordination capacity; each acts
   through a sustainable number of significant relations (the app caps it at 150).

### What Social Organizer is in this model

A **social utility** and coordination tool with three functions: it lets people formalise existing
relations by invitation and bilateral acceptance; it combines them into a referential graph; it
transmits the minimum information needed to coordinate a plurality, using two informational
messages, **Need** (amount; urgent or recurring) and **Participation** (readiness to take part).

It does **not**: decide whether a Need is valid, choose who must respond, assign an executor,
create an irrevocable obligation, enforce performance, perform calculations for people, transfer or
retain resources. Verification, agreement and action happen between people outside the utility.

**Zero direct operational harm.** No operation permitted in the app (formalising a relation,
issuing a Need, indicating Participation, receiving a notification, confirming a completed action)
reduces anyone's resources or alternatives without that person's own further decision. A false
Need can mislead someone's judgment, but any loss follows from an independent action outside the
app. A fake account cannot connect unilaterally: entry needs bilateral acceptance, and then it is
visible on the accepting person's referential path.

**No centre.** The process is a plurality of direct interactions, so there is no single
organisational object that could be captured, prohibited or destroyed as its centre. Switching off
one technical means can slow the messages but does not remove the relations.

**Archive of fulfilled Participation.** Confirmed completed actions are kept; the archive creates
no negative record, sanction or restriction (in the app: "Marked as received by authors").

### Practical deployment

Start in **existing small communities**: they already have significant relations, shared context
and recurring Needs. As members formalise further relations, the graph extends beyond the first
community without central expansion.
