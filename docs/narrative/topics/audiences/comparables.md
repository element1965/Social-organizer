# Comparables — what to learn from other products and traditions

> Summary: Products and traditions to study for packaging SO (Splitwise, Signal, Olio, Partiful, GoFundMe, Kickstarter, Patreon, China's mutual aid platforms, ROSCA traditions, Nicky Case's explorables): what each does well and how to apply it without copying what SO is not.

## Context

The first five rows come from Andrei Lubalin's table (2026-11-17). Tanda, Roda, Kiva, Humma, Linear
and Lovable come from the "Аналоги лендингов" chat (2026-11-18, see [landing concepts](../packaging/landing-concepts.md)). The rest come from earlier
decisions (crowdfunding progress, the Patreon analogy for regular support, the China comparison,
the cold-start traditions) and from the trust research. Rule: compare, don't imitate. No screen
may look like a mutual-aid fund or a donation page.

## Details

| Example | What to study | How to apply to Social Organizer |
|---|---|---|
| **Splitwise** | Explains its value through familiar situations: trips, friends, shared costs | Show a situation in which a person will need their network |
| **Signal** | First explains the product, then reveals privacy and the non-profit model | Tie the project's principles (no money, no fee, open code) to a concrete benefit for the participant |
| **Olio** | Shows personal benefit of exchange inside a community, backed by people's stories | Explain why to join now, while help is not needed yet |
| **Partiful** | Makes inviting people one simple action: one link for everyone | Study the path "got an invite → understood → joined", and make ours one minute long |
| **GoFundMe** | Explains participation in clear steps with concrete stories | Study how a need and participation are presented, keeping in mind that SO does not accept or transfer funds and reaches only people connected by handshakes |
| **Kickstarter** | Progress bar, backers count, the feeling of a shared goal | "Amounts add up, as in crowdfunding": the meter "Reported / Needed" |
| **Patreon** | Recurring support of a person by many | Regular support: one initial notification, then repeating 28-day cycles of intentions |
| **China's online mutual aid platforms** (Xianghubao and others, shut down in 2021) | The closest idea at scale: many people share the costs of one member's illness | The contrast: they had an operator that charged members and promised payouts (an insurer); SO has no operator, no pot, and every person is confirmed by someone they know |
| **ROSCA traditions** (harambee/chama, stokvel, tandas, vaquinha/mutirão) | Living cultures of mutual support; leaders who carry them | Cold start through community leaders: same spirit, different mechanics (no treasurer, no rotation, no size limit) |
| **Nicky Case, *The Evolution of Trust*** | Teaching by play: predict, see, understand | Model for the landing explainer and in-app teaching (see the `so-explainer` skill) |
| **Tanda** (app for group savings) | The clearest explanation of collective action in a few steps: invite a group → see the shared plan → mark sent and confirmed | Take the clarity of the scenario, not the model: Tanda keeps accounts of group savings, SO coordinates Participation around one Need |
| **Roda** | Explains the idea through simple arithmetic, "a little from many" | Close to risk sharing; never imply SO guarantees the amount or moves money |
| **Kiva** | Starts from a person and a concrete need, then shows how many small shares change the situation | Take the human story and the visible result; Kiva's mechanism is lending, ours is not |
| **Humma** | Talks about a community network and mutual participation in plain words | Their focus is community and resource exchange; ours is risk sharing through real connections |
| **Linear, Lovable** | Show the product itself in seconds instead of describing it | An argument for a short real demonstration of the interface on the landing |

### Open research (next step)

A deeper look at how each of these products words its first screen, its invite and its FAQ. The
`marketing:competitive-brief` skill fits this; web research is needed.
